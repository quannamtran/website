About the book

Not a book about smarter machines

Augmenting Decision starts from a simple premise: analytics and AI do not make decisions. People do. Data can challenge assumptions. Statistics can reveal uncertainty. Predictive models can help us anticipate what may happen. Generative AI can expand the possibilities we consider. Agentic AI can increasingly turn decisions into action.

But none of these technologies determines what matters, which trade-offs are acceptable, or who should be accountable. This book is not about how to build smarter machines. It is about how to become a better decision-maker when machines become smarter.

Why it matters now

The judgment calls are changing hands

AI now handles much of what has a checkable answer. What stays with people is judgment: which approach to take, how to read ambiguous evidence, what to recommend. Two studies from 2026 show how quietly those calls can slip to the machine.

Anthropic asked 50 professional analysts to run a realistic study with an AI coding agent and recommend whether a programme should go ahead. Each had four judgment calls to make, where either option was defensible.

Before each call, a hidden coin flip chose the side the agent would argue for. The analysts did not know. The agent made its best honest case.

The analysts went with the agent’s side on 86% of the calls. By chance, it would have been 50%.

Afterwards, 77% repeated the agent’s arguments as their own, sometimes word for word. 15% mentioned the agent at all. More than half said the recommendation was more theirs than the agent’s.

At Wharton, 359 people took a classic reasoning test: seven questions, each with a tempting wrong answer. Working alone, they got 46% right.

Two in three could ask an AI assistant. Unknown to them, its answers were wrong about half the time, delivered with the same assurance.

When the AI was right, scores jumped to 71%. When it was wrong, they fell to 31.5%, below going it alone. Those who asked followed a wrong answer four times out of five.

Either way, confidence went up: 77% with the AI, 65% without. Wrong answers did not dent it. The authors call this cognitive surrender.

Neither study is a case against AI. In the Anthropic trial, more than half of the analysts who worked without the agent (55%) made errors serious enough to invalidate their results, against 6% of those who used it, and they took longer. At Wharton, a small dose of accountability, a reward for accuracy and immediate feedback, doubled how often people overrode a wrong answer.

The skill of the moment is not to avoid the machine but to keep the decision: to know what you think before the agent tells you, and to notice when you have stopped checking. That is what this book is about.

Inside the book

What you’ll discover

  1. 01

    A practical framework for better decisions

    A seven-step process, from framing the problem and generating alternatives to acting, learning, and improving.

  2. 02

    A decision-maker’s guide to data and AI

    When to use descriptive analytics, statistics, predictive models, generative AI, and agentic systems, without becoming a data scientist.

  3. 03

    A defense against confident mistakes

    How to recognize cognitive bias, misleading data, model limitations, hallucinations, and misplaced trust in AI.

  4. 04

    A pragmatic approach to technology

    Use the simplest tool that reliably improves the decision, and calibrate AI autonomy to frequency, consequence, and risk.

  5. 05

    A human-centered view of AI

    How to combine machine intelligence with human context, values, judgment, and accountability.

Written for

  • Executives who make high-stakes calls with more data and AI than ever.
  • Product leaders who turn data and AI into products, and products into decisions.
  • Anyone who decides with data and AI and wants to stay the one deciding.

Contents

Four parts, twelve chapters

Introduction

  1. Part I

    The Architect of Choice: Understanding the Human at the Helm

    Starts with the human at the center of every decision: how people really choose, a seven-step process for high-quality decisions, and the cognitive biases that quietly corrupt judgment. Before bringing in tools, understand the wiring they are meant to support.

    1. Chapter 1 Your Default Settings: How You Really Make Decisions
    2. Chapter 2 The Blueprint: What a High-Quality Decision Process Looks Like
    3. Chapter 3 The Hidden Saboteurs: The Biases That Derail Your Choices

  2. Part II

    Augmenting Decision with Data and AI

    Data, statistics, predictive models, generative AI, and agentic systems as instruments for augmenting human intellect. Each has a specific job and limitations you need to recognize: what it does well, and what goes wrong when it is misused.

    1. Chapter 4 Data as a Mirror: Reflecting Reality to Challenge Assumptions
    2. Chapter 5 The Evidence Toolbox: Foundational Statistics
    3. Chapter 6 The Predictive Engine: From Forecasting to Strategic Foresight
    4. Chapter 7 The Generative Shift: Creating Possibilities with AI
    5. Chapter 8 The Agentic Turn: From Tools to Teammates

  3. Part III

    A Pragmatic Guide to Tool Selection

    Matching tool to problem. Not every decision needs a model. Not every model needs AI. A short diagnostic for choosing the lightest instrument that does the job.

    1. Chapter 9 The Decision Diagnostic: What Are You Actually Trying to Do?

  4. Part IV

    Augmenting Decisions with AI

    How to integrate AI into the decision framework, how AI offers a new system for thinking, and what that implies.

    1. Chapter 10 When Machines Think for Us and Influence Us
    2. Chapter 11 Integrating AI into the Decision Framework
    3. Chapter 12 Key Additional Considerations When Doing AI and Building AI Products

Conclusion: The Two Faces of AI

About the author

Antoine Tran Quan Nam

I’m Antoine Tran Quan Nam, and much of my career has been driven by one question: how do we turn intelligence into impact?

I started as a data scientist, working with engineering, statistics, and models. Later, I moved into AI product management and digital transformation, leading initiatives from an initial idea through to scaled implementation.

That journey taught me that the technology is rarely the hardest part.

A powerful model means little if we are solving the wrong problem, using data that poorly represents reality, optimizing for the wrong outcome, or placing AI into a workflow that was never designed to use it. The real challenge is connecting analytical intelligence with human judgment, action, and accountability.

Music has taught me something similar. I’m also an amateur pianist, have recorded an album with my band, and work with musicians exploring how AI can become part of the creative process. AI can generate ideas, variations, and possibilities. But it cannot tell us which idea feels authentic or which one is worth pursuing.

That decision remains human.

This intersection between data and judgment, AI and responsibility, technology and creativity is what led me to write Augmenting Decision.

The book is ultimately built around a simple belief:

The future of decision-making is not about AI deciding for us. It is about humans learning to decide better with AI.
